### How does "Computer Engineering" differ from "Software Engineering"?

Professional training in computer engineering means knowledge and practical skills in designing the software of computer systems and electronics (the hardware of computer systems and networks). Computer engineers design hardware and software complexes, ranging from microprocessor-based embedded systems to supercomputers, high-performance high-availability systems and cloud platforms.

Software engineering studies methods for the analysis, design, implementation and testing of software, based on the mathematical apparatus of discrete mathematics, algorithmic algebras, functional analysis, etc. Software engineers develop models and methods of software development, from theory to the practice of creating software tools.

The field of computer engineering focuses not only on individual aspects of programming, computer systems and networks, but also on their integration into existing IT infrastructures.

The field of software engineering focuses on the theory and practice of the software development process, in most cases without being tied to existing IT infrastructures and solutions.

### What jobs do the department's graduates do?

Graduates work as programmers and software test engineers, in particular in the fields of embedded devices, high-performance computing and network applications.

### Advice for applicants and students

A classical education is essential for engineers, as it provides valuable critical thinking and analytical skills that are necessary for solving complex technical problems. Engineers must be able to analyse different possible solutions and choose the most effective one. In addition, they must have the mathematical skills provided by a classical education in order to be able to calculate complex systems and loads on structures.

I gained many applied skills while studying at the Department of Computer Engineering: programming languages of different levels, algorithms, the foundations of artificial intelligence, the principles of computer networks, etc. However, the field of information technology is developing at a rapid pace. You should not think that learning ends the moment you receive your diploma. "Learning how to learn" is, in my opinion, one of the most important achievements of the department's graduates. This is ensured both at the basic level, when completing the educational programmes, and at the advanced level, when working on the department's research programmes.

Yes, graduates may have to work with programming languages other than those they used during their studies, or with different hardware, but the approaches and the ability to find critical points and focus on what matters most will stay with you forever. For example, throughout my career I have repeatedly used the knowledge of discrete mathematics gained during my studies, not only to simplify program code, but also to uncover hidden business logic rules. Each time, this gave me a competitive advantage.

My PhD studies and dissertation defence at the Department of Computer Engineering laid a solid foundation for my career development. Our teachers are true professionals in their field, able to prepare students for the challenges that await them in the modern world.
